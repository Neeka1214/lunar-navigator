The problem & our approach

At the lunar south pole, the Sun skims the horizon

Real sky panorama from the Artemis III "Connecting Ridge" region. The Sun (yellow) stays about 1.5° high and slides along the real terrain horizon (grey); Earth (blue) bobs above and below it.
☀

Power: with the Sun only 1–2° high, a hill several km away can block it for days.

🌍

Comms: Earth sits on the horizon and rises and sets as the Moon wobbles (libration).

⏱

Replanning: when a launch slips, every site and date must be checked again, quickly.

👥

Users: mission planners, rover teams, students and anyone who loves looking at the Moon.

Tab 5 · 🐰 Chang'e & Jade Rabbit & Tab 6 · 📚 Learn why

A Moon legend everyone knows makes the science stick

Chang'e's flight calculator, using today's real Earth–Moon distance from JPL Horizons. It can be switched off with one button.

嫦娥奔月: how long would Chang'e take? Walking 9.1 years, Taiwan High Speed Rail 55.2 days, Apollo 11 3.1 days, light 1.32 s.

"But wait, it is not that simple": the Moon moves at 3,680 km/h and won't wait for her; she needs 11 km/s to escape Earth; the distance changes from 356,648 to 406,603 km (48.4 to 55.3 days by train).

🐇 Game: find the Jade Rabbits

5 rabbits hide within 4 km in the 3D walk. Three hide in the darkest, coldest spots from our data. Finding one teaches why water ice survives in permanent shadow.

📚 Learn why

Why the Sun is so low (1.5° tilt), why km matter, libration, the 29.5-day lunar day, no-atmosphere shadows, and the CSA lunar rover and Artemis II.

Trustworthy numbers, from hours of analysis to seconds

agreement with NASA Moon Trek's LOLA permanent-shadow map at 300 random south-pole points (149/150 shadowed, 147/150 sunlit found).

< 0.01°

difference between our Sun and Earth positions and direct JPL Horizons topocentric queries for the same site.

24 sites

official sites from NASA Moon Trek, each with LROC NAC image coverage from QuickMap, and 19,009 hourly steps of data (2026-11 → 2028-12).

Impact

  • Planners: when a launch slips, see at once which sites, dates and nearby spots still give power and comms
  • Rover teams (e.g. CSA's lunar rover): how far to drive for better sunlight
  • Classrooms & the public: feel why the south pole is so hard, and so exciting

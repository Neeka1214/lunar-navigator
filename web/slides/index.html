The problem

At the lunar south pole, the Sun skims the horizon

Real view from the Artemis III "Connecting Ridge" region: the Sun (yellow) stays about 1.5° high and slides along the LOLA terrain horizon. Earth (blue) bobs above and below it.

Power: a hill a few km away can block the Sun for days.

Comms: Earth rises and sets as the Moon wobbles (libration).

Replanning: when a launch slips, NASA's detailed tools are slow to compare many site-and-date options.

Our solution

Pick a site, pick a date, get the answer

Sky panorama + 30-day timeline: sunlit, Earth visible, both.
  • 24 official sites from NASA Moon Trek (13 Artemis III regions, 7 CLPS missions, plus ISRO, JAXA and Apollo), or click anywhere
  • Instant verdict: GONO COMMSNO POWER
  • Time until sunset or Earthset, longest darkness, solar-panel output
  • Landing-window finder: dates with Sun and Earth in view for N or more days

How NASA data makes it possible

Every number comes from NASA open data

JPL Horizons

Hourly Sun and Earth geometry for 2026–2028 (sub-solar, sub-Earth and sub-Moon points). This gives the Sun and Earth position from any lunar site.

LRO LOLA topography

80 m and 240 m terrain models give the real horizon (no atmosphere, with lunar curvature), the heatmaps and the walkable 3D terrain.

NASA Moon Trek

The Artemis III candidate regions, the CLPS landing sites, and the LOLA permanently-shadowed-regions map.

LROC QuickMap

LRO NAC image coverage for every site: how many images, the sharpest one, the newest one.

Space agency partners

CSA Moon exploration (education), ISRO Chandrayaan-3 and JAXA SLIM comparison sites.

Validated

98.7% agreement with Moon Trek's permanent-shadow map (300 test points). Sun and Earth positions match Horizons to within 0.01°.
